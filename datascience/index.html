Now is the time for data science.

Four threats a Head of Data Science will hear in the next quarter and what to say back, collaboratively, with the numbers honest and the judo turning the conversation into a shared win.

The thread through all four threats. Every AI, GenAI and data decision at this company eventually runs through one capability: knowing whether the numbers, the models and the outputs can be trusted. That capability is data science. Cut it and every other function is making product decisions on numbers no one has audited. Fund it and every other function gets the answers it has been asking for. That is the case to make in every conversation below.

1. The Product Manager "Do we even need data science in the loop on every iteration?"

The vague line

"Honestly, with Claude Code and Cursor I think Product can prototype the AI features directly. Do we really need data science in the loop on every iteration, it feels like it's slowing us down."

What's actually being said

"I want the AI-platform brief. My demo runs faster than your evaluation pipeline."

"Product owns prototype speed. Data science owns the evaluation that turns a prototype into a feature the company can actually release. Every AI claim in this app reaches the user through that loop, or it reaches the user untested. There is no third option, and the app store does not give second chances."

You say, the long form

"Agreed, and there is speed to be found on every side of this. Product can move faster by writing specifications that are precise enough for an AI evaluation to run against: clear acceptance criteria, named failure modes, a definition of done that is not 'it looks good in the demo.' My team can move faster by publishing evaluation templates in advance so Product is not waiting on us to start the gate. Both sides have work to do. That is not in dispute."

"The concern is what the company hands legal, the app store reviewer or the user when a live feature output is wrong. The 2024–25 record of shipped AI features is unambiguous: when they fail, they fail in production, not in the demo. For an app company that means a one-star wave, a platform compliance flag or a refund storm before the engineering team has even noticed. Moffatt v. Air Canada[s-21] made the airline liable for chatbot output the airline did not design. Klarna[s-22] rolled customer-service AI back after the failure shipped to users. Stack Overflow's 2025 survey[s-25] shows around two-thirds of professional developers naming 'almost right, but not quite' as the top frustration with AI output. 'Almost right' is exactly the failure mode that survives a demo and dies in production, and the only way to catch it before the user does is an evaluation harness data science owns end-to-end."

"The split I'd propose isn't 'data science gates Product.' It's the opposite: Product owns iteration speed and owns the quality of the brief that makes iteration meaningful, my team owns the one artefact no model vendor can ship us because it would benchmark them: the pre-shipment evaluation of how the feature behaves on this app, this user base, this data. That artefact is not optional, and there is no second team in the company that can produce it. Same speedometer, different lap."

Judo, work with the PM

"Want to co-own this? You set the iteration cadence, I'll bring the eval. When Legal or the COO start asking 'how do we know it's safe to ship,' we both walk in with the same answer and Product keeps the speed credit. Half-day this week to sketch it together?"

2. The CFO "I don't see the GenAI ROI line."

The vague line

"I'm looking at the budget and I'm not seeing the GenAI ROI line yet. Are we double-paying, Cursor licences plus data-science seats, for the same output?"

What's actually being said

"The line item has to justify itself or it gets cut at midyear review."

"Claude Code subscriptions are a rounding error. The line that matters is the cost of a GenAI mistake at scale, in production, in front of users. Data science is the only function that sizes that number before it lands, and the only one that can tell you which AI bet across departments is actually paying back. Without that function, every AI invoice gets signed on faith."

You say, the long form

"Yes, and 'where's the ROI' is the right question, because the 2024–25 evidence is that GenAI ROI is real but concentrated, not absent. BCG's impact gap[s-16], McKinsey's State of AI[s-15] and Deloitte's quarterly tracker[s-17] all say the same thing in different words: the minority of firms capturing measurable P&L value share one thing, internal infrastructure to know which AI investment moves which KPI. For an app company the KPIs are concrete, retention, conversion, ARPU, support cost per active user, refund rate, app store rating. Mapping each AI spend line to one of those is not a finance task and it is not a product task. It is a data-science task, and there is no other team in the company set up to do it. Goldman's too much spend, too little benefit read[s-13] and Brynjolfsson, Rock & Syverson's J-curve work[s-12] name the same gap from the capex side."

"Where I'd be honest with you: the line item to compare isn't 'our data-science payroll vs. Claude Code subscriptions.' Claude Code subscriptions are a few hundred dollars per head per year, they will never dominate the ledger. The line that matters is cost of GenAI mistakes at scale: incident remediation, regulatory exposure (the Air Canada precedent[s-21]), a platform-level rating collapse, reputational reversal (Klarna[s-22]) and product decisions made on hallucinated inputs. Gartner forecasts around 40% of agentic-AI projects cancelled by 2027[s-18]; the cancellation cost is the figure the company should be sizing, and data science is the only function whose job description is sizing it before it lands on your desk as a write-down."

Judo, work with the CFO

"Let me be your measurement arm. Next time another department comes asking for more AI budget, I can give you a register of what is actually working, sized by failure cost. That gives you a defensible answer that is not 'no.' Want me to build the first version against your existing capex template?"

3. The COO "Every other team has shipped an AI feature this quarter."

The vague line

"Every other team has shipped an AI feature this quarter. Data science always seems to be the bottleneck. Can we get this moving?"

What's actually being said

"The CEO and the board are asking me to be fast. You are visibly not making me fast."

"The feeling is right, the current process works for no one. Data science is the function that measures whether a team actually shipped or merely felt like it did, and we are already fixing that. In 30 days I will give you felt-vs-measured cycle time across every team. Without that number, your bottleneck conversations are decided by whoever is loudest in the room, not by what actually held."

You say, the long form

"Yes, my team has to move faster too, and I'd rather that be a measured statement than a vibe. Here's the bit that doesn't make it into status updates: the cleanest randomised evidence available on AI-assisted work, METR's 2025 trial of experienced open-source developers[s-24], found that participants felt they were about 20% faster while measurably being somewhat slower. The sample is narrow and the authors are careful about generalising, but the perception-vs-measurement gap is the part that holds in every other 2025 study I can point to. Stack Overflow's developer survey[s-25] shows trust in AI output falling, not rising, year-on-year. The gap between felt-speed and measured-speed only closes when someone is paid to measure it, and in this company that someone is data science."

"What that means for your question is operational: 'who shipped fast this quarter' and 'who shipped correctly fast this quarter' are different leaderboards, and for an app company the second leaderboard shows up in retention curves, crash rates, support-ticket volume and store ratings two quarters later. The company won't know which leaderboard it is on until then. I'd rather we not find out the hard way. What I can do, and what no other function in the company is set up to do, is run the measurement: believed vs. measured cycle time, by team, by feature, tied to live app metrics for AI-assisted work. That converts 'data science is the bottleneck' from a meeting impression into a number you can defend upwards, and it names the team that owns the number."

Judo, work with the COO

"Co-sponsor the cycle-time instrument with me. When Product claims they shipped fast and you need to show what actually held in operations, you and I walk into that conversation with the same chart. Your name on the methodology, mine on the maths. Coffee Thursday to scope it?"

4. The CEO "What's our AI-first transformation story?"

The vague line

"I want us to be an AI-first company by the end of FY. I keep hearing data science is the 'old paradigm.' What's our transformation story?"

What's actually being said

"The board is asking me a narrative question. I will reward whoever helps me answer it. I would prefer the answer not require me to defend a cost line."

"AI-first is not a vendor choice, it is an in-house discipline. Every firm is buying the same tools. The gap is whether the company's data is clean, connected and trustworthy enough to make those tools useful for Product, Operations and the board. Data science is the team that builds that layer and keeps it honest, for everyone in the room. Without it, AI-first is just AI-spending."

You say, the long form

"Yes, and the transformation story I can put in front of the board with evidence behind it has a sharper edge than the vendor pitch. The 2024–25 record on enterprise GenAI converges on one finding across McKinsey[s-15], BCG[s-16] and Deloitte[s-17]: value is captured by the minority of firms that built an internal measurement and evaluation layer. Gartner's around-40% cancellation forecast[s-18] is the counter-image. Goldman[s-13] calls it 'too much spend, too little benefit' from the capex side. The Quarterly Journal of Economics result on senior-executive miscalibration[s-23] (executives sit inside their own 80% confidence intervals only about a third of the time) and the Microsoft/CMU result on AI users gaining confidence without gaining accuracy[s-20] are why this matters: an AI-first product without measurement is just a more confident product."

"'AI-first' as the board hears it is a vendor-selection story. 'AI-first' as our 2026 board needs to hear it is: we are the firm in the winning quartile because we built the in-house data and evaluation layer that makes those tools actually pay back on retention, conversion and the cost of running the app. That doesn't make my team the old paradigm. It puts data science on the board slide as the only function whose job is turning AI spend into measurable value for the user and the product, with my team holding the pen on the numbers Product, Operations and Finance are already asking for."

Judo, work with the CEO

"Let me draft your next board AI slide with you. Three named bets, pre-registered metrics, named human owners, you walk in with my team's numbers, the vendors and analysts don't get to set the narrative for the company. Want a first pass before the next prep session?"

If all four speak in the same meeting

"All four of these questions converge on one answer. Product is asking how fast can we ship. Finance is asking is the spend working. Operations is asking why aren't we shipped already. The board is asking what's our story. Each of you needs the same thing: a trustworthy reading of whether the company's AI, GenAI and data investments are producing something measurable for the user, the ledger and the app. Retention, conversion, support cost, app store rating, refund rate. Those numbers do not produce themselves and they do not interpret themselves. They live inside one function. Data science is the only team in this company whose entire output is evaluating, measuring and improving how AI turns into product decisions that move those numbers. The 2025 enterprise surveys say the firms capturing GenAI value built that capability in-house[s-15][s-16][s-17]; the firms in the cancellation cohort did not[s-18]. Cut data science and every other function is making AI claims it cannot defend, and the app ships features no one has audited. Fund data science and every other function gets the numbers it has been asking for, and the app ships features with evidence behind them. That is not a bid for more headcount, it is a statement of which function the AI strategy already depends on, whether or not the room has named it."

In every case the threat dissolves the moment data science is reframed as the team that helps the person opposite, not the team competing with them. The budget conversation becomes a coalition conversation and the coalition is the win.

That is the operational meaning of now is the time: not a slogan, a calendar slot and one that closes when the board asks the question your peers cannot answer with their own numbers.
